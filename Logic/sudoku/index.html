4 × 4 Sudoku: find what must fit

Every row, column, and bold 2 × 2 box must contain 1, 2, 3, and 4 exactly once. Sudoku is not guessing: collect what is missing, then find the only legal home.

Use the SCAN method

S

Scan three directions

Look across the row, down the column, and inside the bold box.

C

Collect missing numbers

Write the numbers from 1–4 that have not appeared yet.

A

Ask where each can go

Cross out a square when its row, column, or box already has that number.

N

Note the single and repeat

Place a number only when one choice remains, then SCAN again.

How to think before placing a number

1. Start where the information is strongest

Do not begin with the first empty square. Look for a row, column, or box that already contains two or three numbers. Fewer missing numbers means fewer possibilities to compare.

2. Separate “could fit” from “must fit”

A number may be legal in two squares. That is not enough to place it. Keep both possibilities open until another row, column, or box blocks one of them.

3. Explain every crossed-out choice

Say the reason precisely: “3 cannot go here because this column already has a 3.” If no group blocks the candidate, do not cross it out.

4. Let each answer create the next clue

After placing one number, immediately rescan its row, column, and box. One placement changes three groups and often creates a new single.

Recognize two kinds of “only choice”

Square single: one empty square has only one legal candidate after checking its row, column, and box.

Group single: a missing number has only one possible square inside a row, column, or box, even if that square has other candidates.

Think aloud: “This group is missing ___. Candidate ___ is blocked here by ___. Therefore it must go ___.”

If stuck: move to a different group, list candidates lightly, and search for a new single. Being stuck means “look elsewhere,” not “guess.”

Worked example: click each deduction

Start by scanning

Do not look at the whole puzzle at once. Find a row, column, or box with only one or two blanks.

Parent prompt: “What numbers are missing? Which square is blocked, and by what?”

Try the same puzzle yourself

Choose an empty square, then choose a number.

What to think before writing

  1. Say the missing numbers out loud.
  2. Test one candidate against the row, column, and box.
  3. If two candidates still work, leave the square blank.
  4. After every placement, scan again because new singles may appear.
  5. At the end, check that every group reads 1, 2, 3, 4.
