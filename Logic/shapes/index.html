Shape-Number Card Detective

Learn to solve double-sided card puzzles by tracking what stays the same, building an inventory of clues, and eliminating impossible hidden sides.

2. Use the SAME method on any problem

These are not arithmetic problems. They are “same objects, different view” puzzles. Imagine that the two rows are photographs of the same physical cards taken before and after someone flipped some cards and shuffled them.

S

Same cards

Count the physical cards. The second picture must contain exactly the same cards as the first picture.

A

Arrange clues

Make a shape inventory and a number inventory. Count repeated symbols instead of recording only unique symbols.

M

Mark changes

Notice what disappeared, what appeared, what is visible at the same time, and which cards must have been flipped.

E

Eliminate

Cross out impossible hidden sides. When only one candidate remains, use the leftover and check it against both pictures.

Hidden-side questions

Build the complete shape inventory. Remove the shapes already visible beside the target number. The remaining shape is hidden behind the target.

Disappearing-symbol questions

If a number disappears, its card was flipped. Its opposite shape must be visible in the other picture. Narrow the visible shapes until one candidate remains.

Counting-property questions

Reconstruct the complete number inventory first. Then count the requested property, such as odd numbers, even numbers, or numbers greater than a value.

3. Coach the reasoning without giving the answer

Ask one question at a time and let the child point to the evidence:

  1. How many physical cards are there?
  2. What can change: the card itself, its position, or the side we see?
  3. Should we match cards by position after they were mixed?
  4. Which shapes and numbers can we prove exist?
  5. Are any identical symbols visible at the same time?
  6. Which symbol disappeared after the cards were flipped?
  7. Which candidates can we cross out, and why?
  8. What is the only possibility left?
  9. Does that answer work in both pictures?
“Ignore where the cards are. Count what must exist, cross out what cannot fit, and use what is left.”

Try a physical demonstration

  1. Make three or four index cards. Draw one shape on the front and write one number on the back.
  2. Lay them in a row and ask the child to describe only what is visible.
  3. Have the child close their eyes while you flip some cards and shuffle the row.
  4. Ask, “What changed?” and “What stayed exactly the same?”
  5. Repeat until the child automatically says, “The positions changed, but the physical cards stayed the same.”

4. Worked worksheet solutions

Problem 29 — What shape is behind 4?

  1. There are three cards, so there are exactly three shape sides.
  2. Across the two pictures, the three shape sides are triangle, square, and circle.
  3. When 4 is visible, triangle and square are visible on the other two cards.
  4. Those two shapes cannot also be behind 4.
  5. The only unused shape is the circle.

Answer: the other side of 4 is a circle.

Problem 30 — What shape is behind 1?

  1. The 1 appears in the first picture but disappears in the second.
  2. The physical card cannot disappear, so the 1-card must have been flipped to its shape side.
  3. The only shape visible in the second picture is a triangle.
  4. Therefore, that triangle must be the flipped 1-card.

Answer: the other side of 1 is a triangle.

Problem 31 — What shape is behind 9?

  1. Two triangles are visible at the same time, so two different cards have triangle shape sides.
  2. The other picture also reveals a circle and a square.
  3. The complete shape inventory is triangle, triangle, circle, and square.
  4. When 9 is visible, one triangle, the circle, and the square are visible on the other cards.
  5. The unused item from the inventory is the second triangle.

Answer: the other side of 9 is a triangle.

Problem 32 — How many cards have an odd number?

  1. The four visible shapes across the pictures are square, triangle, circle, and star. They account for all four cards.
  2. In the first picture, the circle-card and star-card are showing the numbers 3 and 1.
  3. In the second picture, the square-card and triangle-card are showing the numbers 2 and 1.
  4. The two visible 1s belong to different cards because they come from different shape groups.
  5. The complete number inventory is 3, 1, 2, and 1.
  6. The odd numbers are 3, 1, and 1.

Answer: three of the four cards have an odd number.

5. Common mistakes to watch for

  • Matching columns: after mixing, the first card in one picture is not necessarily the first card in the other picture.
  • Ignoring duplicates: two triangles visible simultaneously prove that two different triangle cards exist.
  • Assuming matching symbols identify one card: the same number can appear on more than one physical card.
  • Pairing too early: build the full shape or number inventory before deciding which sides belong together.
  • Doing unnecessary arithmetic: most questions use counting, comparison, and elimination rather than calculation.
  • Skipping the check: pretend to flip and shuffle the proposed cards; the answer must be consistent with both pictures.
